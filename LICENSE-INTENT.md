# License Intent & Educational Values

## Why MIT?

MultiController is released under the **MIT License** because we believe in:
- **Maximum accessibility** — educators and students everywhere should be able to use this tool without restriction
- **Legal simplicity** — MIT is widely understood and trusted by the open source community
- **Community collaboration** — we want contributions from people building on this work

## Why This Statement?

While MIT places no legal restrictions on commercial use, we want to be transparent about our **actual intent and values**. This document exists to explain what MultiController is *for*, even though the license allows other uses.

## Our Core Mission

**MultiController exists to rescue retiring LEGO Education sets and make sensor robotics accessible to every classroom.**

LEGO Education kits are expensive (~$200–500 per hub). When they're discontinued and sensors become unavailable, perfectly good hardware becomes waste. MultiController lets educators:
- Give old LEGO hubs new life with modern M5Stack sensors
- Keep their classroom investment useful for years longer
- Teach engineering and science without licensing costs
- Experiment freely without fear of breaking expensive original sensors

## Who This Is For

### 🎓 Educators (Our Primary Audience)
- Use MultiController freely in your classroom
- Modify it for your students' needs
- Share your improvements with other teachers
- You'll never be charged for core features

### 🏫 Educational Institutions
- Deploy to multiple classrooms without licensing fees
- Adapt the firmware to your curriculum
- Use as a platform for student sensor projects

### 👨‍💼 Commercial Users
You're **welcome to use this** in your product. We ask that you:
1. **Credit the project** — mention MultiController in your documentation and acknowledge the MIT license
2. **Support continued development** — if your product succeeds commercially, consider supporting the project:
   - **Buy Me a Coffee** donations fund hardware and sensors for new driver development
   - **GitHub Sponsors** enables ongoing maintenance and feature improvements
   - **Direct donations** for specific sensor support (e.g., "I need AS7341 spectral sensor support")
   - **Code contributions** are always welcome (sensor drivers, bug fixes, documentation)
3. **Respect the open source community** — don't remove attributions or claim original authorship
4. **Optional: Share improvements** — if you've added sensor drivers or features, consider sharing via pull request so other educators benefit

### 🤝 Researchers & Hobbyists
- Build on this work freely
- Share your innovations
- You're part of our community

### 🌍 Non-Profits & NGOs
- Use for free (MIT allows it anyway, but we want to be clear: **go ahead**)
- Educational mission? We'll help however we can

## What We Hope Won't Happen

We trust the community, but we'd discourage:
- **Reselling MultiController unchanged** as a finished product without attribution or added value
- **Removing credits or open-source notices** from derivative works
- **Contradicting the educational mission** — using loopholes in the license to exclude students or educators

That said: **MIT doesn't legally prevent these things**, and we've accepted that trade-off to keep the license simple and permissive. We choose to trust that most people share our values.

## What We Actually Control

As the maintainers, we control:
- The **core repository** — we decide what gets merged
- **Branding** — you can't claim MultiController is your original work
- **Direction** — we'll prioritize educational use cases and community contributions
